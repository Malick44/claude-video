**Snapshot:** September 25, 2026, 17:38 UTC. **Channel:** [@fern-tv](https://www.youtube.com/@fern-tv/videos). **Scope:** 50 most recent regular uploads, September 21, 2025–September 23, 2026. [Video-level data](fern-tv-2026-09-25.csv).

## Repeatable patterns

1. **A weekly documentary slot.** The latest 12 uploads arrived exactly seven days apart on Wednesdays. The median run time across all 50 is 27.6 minutes; the format is a substantial narrated case study rather than a short-form feed.
2. **A clear narrative promise in the title.** 42 of 50 titles start with “The,” “How,” or “Why.” They usually specify a subject plus conflict or unanswered question: an agent infiltrates a group, a creator category dies, a murder remains unsolved, or a company conceals something. The two largest outliers both offer an inside account of a larger institution or system.
3. **Several editorial lanes under one style.** Recent uploads span crime and intelligence, geopolitics, corporate conduct, dark-web stories, and occasional creator-industry commentary. The format stays recognizable even as the subject changes.
4. **Strong visual shorthand.** In a sample of six recent official thumbnails, each has an instantly readable focal subject; many use dark negative space and a red accent. Text is sparse and helps identify the stakes rather than carrying the whole premise. This is a visual observation from a small sample, not a measured performance correlation.
5. **Evidence and monetization are built into the publishing template.** All 50 sampled descriptions contain an ad disclosure and music credit; 49 mention sources. That consistency gives viewers a path to supporting material. Sponsorship is routine, but the placement differs between the three videos reviewed below.

## Ideas to test for a competing channel

- Package one story around a specific person, institution, or object and a question the audience can immediately understand. Test a direct “How [person] [high-stakes action]” title against a “The [institution/system] [hidden failure]” title.
- Use one dominant visual motif in the thumbnail and keep any text brief. Test this as a package with the title, since either asset alone can change the promise.
- Open with a concrete scene or piece of evidence, establish the stakes within the first minute, and make the investigative question explicit. The three reviewed openings use different visual material to do this.
- If producing long-form investigations, publish on a dependable day and show sources in the description. Fern's cadence and sourcing are observable standards; their causal effect on views is not known from this dataset.
- Review results at equal video ages (for example, first 7 days) before deciding which subject lane or package wins. Today's cumulative-view ranking favors older uploads.

## Platform state and limits

Fern is now tracked as `youtube_long / fern-tv` in the local Competitor Intel database, with 50 videos, 50 first-capture metric snapshots, and the platform's view and engagement scores. Open the [Outlier Feed](http://localhost:3000/?window=all) and select `fern-tv` in the creator filter; the [Competitors page](http://localhost:3000/competitors) shows the baseline and subscriber count.

The platform originally ingested YouTube **Shorts** only; a separate YouTube Long option was added after this snapshot. Fern's channel had no Shorts tab at this check. I imported public metadata for its regular videos so the existing database, scoring function, and dashboard could be used. The analysis remains **metrics and editorial review**: the Deepgram/Claude/OpenAI media pipeline did not run because its credentials are not configured. No transcripts, hook frames, beat maps, or embeddings were written to `video_intelligence`. YouTube exposes no share or save counts here, so the platform's engagement score for these uploads uses likes and twice the comment count only.

**Method:** Public metadata was read with `yt-dlp` from the channel's Videos tab and individual watch pages. The CSV records title, published time, duration, views, likes, comments, platform outlier score, and engagement score for each upload. Views, likes, comments, subscriber count, and titles may change after capture. The current baseline is applied retrospectively to older uploads, and no age-normalized view series was available.
